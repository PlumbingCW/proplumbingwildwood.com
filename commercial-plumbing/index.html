When a business plumbing problem cannot wait until morning

Some failures are inconvenient and some stop trading. Treat these as emergencies: water running from a ceiling or pipe you cannot shut off, sewage coming up through a floor drain or toilet, a restaurant kitchen with no working drains, and any leak near electrical panels or equipment.

Close the main shutoff if you can find it, or the valve nearest the leak. Do not walk through standing water near outlets. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before calling a plumber.

Document everything before cleanup begins. Date-stamped photos of the water, the damaged stock or ceiling tiles, and the fixture involved help with an insurance claim or a talk with a landlord. Your policy decides what is covered.

MSD sewer or private treatment: it changes who owns the problem

The city's FAQ says sewer service is MSD Project Clear, but not every Wildwood property sits inside that system. The Master Plan says much of the city's soil is unsuitable for individual septic systems, and that developments outside the sewer district often rely on package treatment plants. It describes most of the Town Center area as inside public sewer and water service areas that still rely in part on individual systems.

City code section 510.080 says a building with plumbing connects to a public sewer when one is available, and a sewer counts as available when a sanitary line is within 200 feet. So confirm early whether your building is on MSD, a private line to a package plant, or its own system.

That answer decides who clears a blockage past your property line and what you can safely send down a drain. Ask your landlord or the building department, then tell us so we scope the job correctly.

Commercial leak detection: what it can and cannot tell you

Finding a leak in a commercial building is a process of narrowing, and it does not always end with a spot you can circle. Tools include a pressure test on isolated branches, acoustic listening on exposed pipe and valves, a line locator for buried pipe, and a camera for drain lines.

We close valves to split the system, test each section, and keep halving until the loss is confined to one stretch of pipe. Under a slab, a parking lot or a finished ceiling, the last step may need an opening, and we will tell you where and why before anything is cut.

Some limits are real. Fire sprinkler lines, irrigation and refrigeration or hydronic loops are often separate systems with their own tradespeople, and we will say so rather than guess. A leak your water utility flagged on the account is worth chasing even if nothing looks wet. Our leak detection page covers the same methods at home scale.

Grease trap cleaning and other commercial drains

A grease trap catches fats, oils and grease before they reach the sewer line. When it fills, kitchen drains slow first, then smell, then back up during the dinner rush. Pump-outs on a regular schedule are cheaper than the emergency version, and we handle both.

How often depends on how much a kitchen fries, bakes and washes. Ask the permitting authority for your site about interceptor size and cleaning records, because requirements differ by building. If you take over a restaurant space, ask for the pump-out history first.

Floor drains, mop sinks and restroom lines need other tools. A cable clears most, and a main that keeps clogging may call for hydro jetting after a camera shows what is in the pipe.

After-hours and weekend work costs more than a scheduled weekday visit.

Access and building size.

Pipe material and location.

Exposed copper behind a wall is faster than a line under a slab or paving.

Diagnostic steps.

Camera work, pressure tests and leak surveys are added when the cause is not obvious.
