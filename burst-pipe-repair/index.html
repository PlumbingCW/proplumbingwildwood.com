A burst pipe is an emergency: the first ten minutes

Do these in order while you wait for a plumber, and stop if anything feels unsafe.

  • Close the main valve.

    Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. It often sits where the line enters the house, and the shutoff at the outdoor meter is a backup.

  • Open the lowest faucet.

    Draining the lines lets standing water leave through the tap and not through the break.

  • Turn off the water heater.

    Switch off the gas control or the breaker so the tank is not heating with water trapped inside.

  • Stay clear of wet electricity.

    If water is near outlets, a panel or a basement ceiling light, do not step into it. Shut off power only if you can reach the breaker while dry.

  • Move belongings and take photos.

    Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor, then photograph the damage and the break for insurance or a landlord.

  • Smell gas? Leave first.

    If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility's emergency line or 911 before calling a plumber.

Frozen pipes and Wildwood cold snaps

Pipes burst when ice forms inside them and builds pressure between the ice plug and a closed tap. The pipe usually fails at a weak spot, not at the ice itself.

The December 2022 cold snap that pushed wind chills far below zero is a recent reminder. St. Louis Public Radio reported a minus 33 degree wind chill that Thursday night. Wind chill measures how cold exposed skin feels, not the temperature of a pipe, but wind does push cold air through gaps at rim joists, vents and garage doors.

The city posted in January 2025 that frozen pipes and other water line problems become more common as temperatures fall, and it listed Missouri American Water main breaks on streets near Lake Chesterfield. Those breaks were in the utility's mains, not house plumbing. If your water stops in cold weather, check your own exposed pipes first, then ask the utility whether there is a street-level problem.

Likely freeze spots are pipes in exterior walls, garages, crawlspaces, uninsulated basement corners and outdoor hose bibs. To thaw a frozen pipe, open the tap, warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, and never use a torch. If a pipe is already split, thawing releases water, so be at the main valve first.

Keep cabinet doors open on cold nights, let a tap drip on exterior-wall lines, and disconnect hoses from outdoor faucets before the first hard freeze.

Copper, PEX and PVC: how each fails and how we fix it

The pipe material decides the repair method and how long the fix takes.

PVC and CPVC are used for different jobs, and we match the fix to the original pipe type.

Copper, PEX and PVC repair, pipe by pipe

Copper pipe repair

A frozen copper line usually splits along a seam, and a worn one fails as a pinhole. We cut back to clean, round copper, remove the damaged length and join new pipe with soldered or press fittings. A repair clamp can stop a leak in a pinch, but it is a patch, not a fix.

PEX pipe repair

PEX is flexible and tolerates more expansion than copper, yet it can still split, and fittings are its weak point. We replace the failed section with a PEX coupling or a new fitting and check the neighboring joints, because one bad crimp often hints at more.

PVC pipe repair

PVC carries cold water, drains and irrigation. It breaks when struck, stressed or left to freeze while full. We cut out the cracked piece, fit a coupling with solvent cement and let it cure before the line is pressurized again. Hot water lines need a different material.

Cracked or leaking pipes that have not burst yet

Not every failure is a gusher. A hairline crack, a weeping joint or a pinhole can drip for weeks and stain a subfloor before anyone looks. A mineral crust on copper, a damp joist below a bathroom and a wet spot that returns after wiping are signs worth a call.

A short repair on a sound pipe is practical. A pipe with several leaks, thin walls or corrosion near fittings is a candidate for replacing the whole run. We show you the old section and explain which situation you have, because patching a failing pipe tends to move the leak down the line.

If the source is not visible, a leak detection visit finds the spot before anyone cuts anything.

Cracked drain pipes

Drain pipe damage looks different from supply damage. You may see sewage-smelling dampness, slow drains in one part of the house, or a stain that appears after the washer discharges. We camera the line to see the crack, then replace the damaged section with matching pipe and the right fittings.

If roots or settling are involved, the repair may run into the sewer line repair scope.

Documenting damage for insurance or a landlord

Take wide and close-up photos before anything is moved, note the date and time you shut off the water, and keep the broken pipe section if we remove it. Save every receipt for repairs and drying equipment.

Renters should tell the landlord or property manager right away, in writing if possible. Your policy decides what is covered, and an adjuster will want the timeline and the pipe.

What affects the price of burst pipe repair

Two burst pipes can look alike and cost very different amounts. You get a clear price before work starts.

Pipe material and size.

Copper and larger lines cost more to fit than short PEX or PVC runs.

Access to the pipe.

A split in an open basement ceiling is faster than one behind finished drywall or in a tight crawlspace.

Length of damaged pipe.

A single pinhole is a short repair, while a freeze that damaged several feet or multiple spots is not.

Time of the call.

After-hours emergency work involves night dispatch, which can price differently from a scheduled visit.

Follow-up work.

Opening a wall may need patching, and drying or restoration is a separate trade.

Sudden burst damage, such as a freeze split that floods a room, is often covered by homeowners insurance. Wear, corrosion and long-term leaks often are not, and some policies exclude vacant homes. Your policy decides, so call your agent early.
