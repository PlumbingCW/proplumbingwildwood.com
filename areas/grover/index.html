Where Grover sits inside Wildwood

Grover grew up around a rural post office, and one was operating there as early as 1888 where Manchester Road and Eatherton Road cross. It is one of the communities that came together when Wildwood incorporated in 1995, and it has no separate city government today.

Old Manchester Road through Grover follows the original alignment of U.S. Route 66. The city's own map places the original City Hall at 16962 Manchester Road and the Metro West Fire Protection District headquarters at 17065 Manchester Road.

We could not find a published boundary for Grover. When you call, give the dispatcher your full street address and the nearest cross street, because a landmark alone can be ambiguous.

Why the sewer question comes first

Wildwood does not run on one uniform system. City code section 510.080 requires a building with plumbing fixtures to connect to a public sewer when one is available, and it treats a sewer as available when a sanitary line is within 200 feet. Properties outside that reach may use a private system.

Before we suggest a repair, we ask whether your address is on a public sewer, a private line to a treatment plant or its own system. That answer decides who owns the line and who pays to fix it. A sewer camera inspection then shows what is actually underground.

What an older area does and does not tell you

The Census American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024 reports a median build year of 1992 for Wildwood as a whole, and about 6.8 percent of homes were built before 1970. The city lists Grover among its historic communities, so some older buildings are likely along Old Manchester Road.

We cannot name a street, and we do not assume cast iron or any other material until a camera or a plumber's inspection shows it. If a building is older, ask for an inspection instead of a guess. If it is newer, the same inspection still helps after a repeated clog.
