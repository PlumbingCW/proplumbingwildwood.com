A city built between 1960 and 1979

Ballwin incorporated on December 29, 1950 with about 750 residents. By 1960 the count was 5,710, and by 1990 it had reached 21,816. The city's 2007 plan, using 2000 Census figures, puts the median year built at 1973.

That plan also shows 27.8 percent of housing built in the 1960s and 31.0 percent in the 1970s, which is 58.8 percent from 1960 to 1979. Over 40 percent of properties were already more than 50 years old when the plan was written. In houses from that period we expect original cast-iron drains, early plastic fittings and a clay lateral that has had years to take on roots.

What the 1990s annexations added

Growth in the 1990s was driven mainly by annexation, which pulled newer large-lot subdivisions into the city. The plan names developments such as Villas at Meadowbrook, Clayton Corners at Clayton Road and Henry Avenue, and Spring Cove at Ries Road and Big Bend Road.

Which streets are oldest is not documented beyond that, so tell dispatch your build decade when you call. A newer house points us toward fixtures and the water heater, and an older one toward drains and the lateral.

The lateral, in the city's own words

Ballwin defines a lateral as the pipe carrying sewage from your house to the main, and the Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District owns that main. Voters approved a $28 annual fee in April 1999. The program reimburses up to $4,500 per job (raised from $3,000), with a $150 application through the MyGov portal.

By the city's account, the likeliest sign of a failing lateral is a backup in the house, often in the basement and first under heavy water use like a washing machine. Clearing roots more than once a year counts as repair, while annual clearing is normal maintenance. The pipe under the house is not covered, and the program is not meant to satisfy a home sale contingency.

Buying or selling in Ballwin

Because the program will not fix a sale contingency, a sewer camera inspection before closing is the practical way to learn what the lateral looks like. Ask for the video and keep it with your closing papers.

Creeks, soils and the November 2024 rain

The city plan says floodplain areas lie mainly along Fishpot Creek, Grand Glaize Creek, Kiefer Creek and the Meramec River. Soils along the Meramec are described as unsuited to built improvements because of how often they flood.

On November 4 to 6, 2024, the National Weather Service logged 8.34 inches of rain in a Ballwin report. Grand Glaize Creek at Valley Park, downstream of Ballwin, crested at 17.33 feet against a 13 foot flood stage. Homes near these creeks should test a sump pump and consider a battery backup.

The Manchester Road corridor

Manchester Road is Ballwin's main commercial corridor, and the city's Olde Towne Plaza is a retail center on it. Vlasis Park, 31 acres off Kehrs Mill Road north of Manchester, hosts Ballwin Days and sits beside the Government Center and Public Works.

Restaurants and shops along the corridor often need commercial plumbing help after closing, when a drain backs up. The city does not offer commercial permits online, so those jobs start with a phone call to City Hall.

Permits through the MyGov portal

Since November 1, 2025, Ballwin requires all contractor and permit applications through its MyGov Permit Portal. Online residential plumbing permits include backflow preventer, plumbing remodel, plumbing repair, single family residence plumbing and water heaters, which has its own permit type. Inspections are scheduled by calling City Hall.

Ballwin also asks contractors to hold a Ballwin licence and an active St. Louis County licence in their field. The city has adopted the International Plumbing Code, 2015 edition under Bill 4004 of 2018, and has been drafting a newer edition. Ask the Inspections Department which edition applies to your job.

Before we arrive: older-house checks

Four steps matched to Ballwin's housing and lateral rules.

1

Find the cleanout.

Locate the sewer cleanout near the foundation or in the yard, but do not open it if it is under pressure. Tell dispatch where it is.

2

Watch the washer.

A lateral backup shows first when the washer drains. Stop laundry and the dishwasher until we have cleared the line.

3

Shut off supply.

Close the main at the meter or where the line enters the house. Original copper and old shutoffs can stick, so turn them gently.

4

Take photos.

Photograph any water damage, the heater data plate and the drain you saw backing up. Your insurance policy decides what is covered.
