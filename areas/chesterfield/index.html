Water from below: the Missouri River floodplain

Chesterfield Valley, a community inside the city limits, has more than 4,500 acres of levee-protected area, according to the city's floodplain page. The Monarch-Chesterfield levee holds back Missouri River floods. Protected ground is still low ground, so drainage depends on pumps and on how well your own equipment works.

That is why a failed sump pump matters so much near the Valley. When the river rises, groundwater tends to rise with it, and a pit that was dry for months can fill quickly. If you manage property there, sump pump repair or a battery backup is worth pricing before the next river warning.

July 30, 1993: when the levee failed

The Monarch levee failed at about 10:15 p.m. on July 30, 1993. Floodwater stood 6 to 15 feet deep across roughly 4,000 acres of the Valley, which had not flooded since 1935 (public radio reporting).

The city says the main levee has since been raised 5 to 7 feet and widened by up to 300 feet. A separate privately maintained agricultural levee to the north was expected to overtop in 2017, and in 2013 it came very close before the city started Valley Watch. The takeaway for an owner is to keep a call plan ready, because protection reduces the risk and does not remove it.

Sewage backing up and the city's lateral program

Chesterfield's sanitary and storm sewers belong to MSD, and the city's Residential Sanitary Sewer Lateral Repair Program is separate from it. Voters approved the program on April 4, 2000, effective January 1, 2001, with a $28 annual fee on the real estate tax bill collected by the county. It targets certain repairs on homes of six units or fewer.

The city page does not list exclusions, so ask City Hall what a given repair would get before you hire a digger. In a backup, sewer line cleaning gets water moving again, and a camera shows whether the pipe needs repair.

Tank heaters past their prime

Chesterfield incorporated in 1988 and had about 40,000 residents by 1993. We have no published median build year for the city, so we cannot say how many homes still have their first heater. The practical rule is to check the data plate and plan for a water heater replacement when a tank passes a decade.

Missouri American Water supplies the city. Its 2023 St. Louis region report printed Missouri River plant hardness of 82 to 226 ppm and Meramec plant hardness of 66 to 150 ppm, but the report is system-wide and names no Chesterfield street. Test your tap, because sediment in a tank is a plausible result of any hard supply.

Permits: the city zones and the county issues

The City of Chesterfield issues a Municipal Zoning Approval, and St. Louis County issues the building permit afterward under a contract with the city. A fire district approval is a separate permit.

The city's page does not address plumbing permits or water heater swaps, so confirm with St. Louis County whether yours needs a permit. We will say what we see before work starts. Licence checks go through the county's plumbing examiners board.

Downtown Chesterfield and the old mall site

The former Chesterfield Mall site, 96 acres, was demolished from October 2024 to summer 2025. The Staenberg Group's planned Downtown Chesterfield project is described at about $2 billion, with up to 2,363 residential units (news report, December 2025). Its current status after that date is unverified.

Large construction projects can disturb water and sewer service nearby. If your water turns cloudy or loses pressure with no leak on your side, call the utility first and then us.

Before we arrive: floodplain and backup checks

Four steps for a flooding or backup call in Chesterfield.

1

Keep clear of electrical.

If water is near a panel or appliances, do not wade in. Leave the area and call us or the utility to make it safe.

2

Check the sump pit.

Look at the pit and the float without touching wiring. Note if the pump runs, hums or is silent, and tell dispatch.

3

Move valuables up.

Lift documents, electronics and boxes above the floor while you wait. Flood coverage is often separate, so call your insurer.

4

Mark the shutoffs.

Know where the main valve and the water heater shutoff are. In a business, tape a note by the valve for after-hours staff.
