When the Meramec comes up

On December 30, 2015, the National Weather Service recorded the Meramec River near Eureka cresting at 46.06 feet. That was a new record, passing the 42.89 feet of December 6, 1982. The same storm put 7.85 inches of rain into the Eureka gauge.

A crest like that pushes groundwater into basements and sewer lines well before water reaches a street. If your home sits low or near the river, test your sump pump now and again when a river warning is issued. Call us the same day if it runs nonstop or does not start.

The city also keeps a floodplain management chapter in its code, so ask before adding a basement fixture in a mapped flood area.

Missouri River water instead of wells

Missouri American Water closed its $28 million purchase of the Eureka water and sewer systems on August 4, 2022. Before that, the supply came from six groundwater wells in the Ozark Aquifer. A roughly 6.3 mile main from Wildwood now brings treated Missouri River water from the utility's Central Plant in Maryland Heights.

The utility publishes 7 to 9 grains per gallon (120 to 150 ppm) for the new supply and says it does not soften water. Scale on heater elements and fixtures is still possible, so a softener or filter question is fair. We cannot know your hardness without a test, so test your tap first.

The disinfectant is now chloramine, which dialysis patients and fish owners must remove. The utility also says customers are responsible for a pressure reducing valve when pressure runs above 80 psi. If your water feels stronger than it used to, we can check it with a gauge.

After a pressure drop or boil advisory

Missouri American Water issued a precautionary boil advisory on April 18, 2024 for part of Eureka north of I-44, caused by low water pressure. Afterward, run cold taps until clear, and call us if a fixture keeps spitting or a heater makes new noises.

Before we arrive: flood and water-source checks

These four steps fit what Eureka homes tend to face.

1

Stop the water at the main.

Find the main shutoff and close it if a pipe is spraying or a fixture will not stop. Open one low faucet to drain the lines.

2

Stay out of basement water.

Standing water near a panel, outlet or appliance can carry current. Leave the area and keep children and pets away until it is safe.

3

Photograph the waterline.

Take dated photos of the water level, the sump pit and any damaged items. These help when you talk to your insurer later.

4

Hold off on discolored water.

If taps run brown after flushing or a pressure change, skip the washer and water heater. Run a cold tap until it clears and note the time.
