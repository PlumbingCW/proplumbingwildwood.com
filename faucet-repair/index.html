Emergency Faucet Repair in Wildwood, MO

A faucet that sprays, a shutoff valve that snaps or a shower that will not turn off can flood a room in minutes. Faucet repair in Wildwood, MO means fixing or replacing the parts that control water at a fixture: cartridges, seats, supply valves, shower valves, tub spouts and outdoor hose bibs.

Urgent situation

When a broken supply valve or faucet is an emergency

Call at once if a supply valve is spraying, a faucet has broken off the sink, or water is dripping through the ceiling below. Close the shutoff valve at the fixture. If it will not turn or the handle snaps, go to the main valve, usually where the water line enters the house, and turn it fully clockwise.

Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain pressure, and switch off power to anything near the water. If the leak is in the wall behind a shower, do not cut into it, because a plumber can often reach the problem from a smaller opening.

We take after-hours and weekend calls on the 24/7 line, and same-day appointments are offered when available. Photograph the damage and keep the broken part, since an insurer or landlord may ask to see it.

Cartridge, ball, disc or compression: what is inside your faucet

Faucets use four basic designs. Compression faucets have separate hot and cold handles and a rubber washer that wears out. Cartridge faucets use a replaceable insert, ball faucets use a slotted ball with rubber seats, and ceramic disc faucets use two plates that slide against each other.

Knowing the type tells us what to bring. A worn cartridge is a fast, low-cost swap if the brand still makes it. Older faucets may have no parts available, and replacement is the more honest answer.

Water chemistry matters too. Missouri American Water's 2023 report lists total hardness of 66 to 150 ppm at its Meramec plants and 82 to 226 ppm at its Missouri River plants, and we cannot say which plant serves your address. Mineral scale clogs aerators and wears seals faster, so test your tap and ask your water utility for its annual water quality report.

Shower valves and tub spouts

Shower trouble usually comes from one of these.

  • Water runs after you shut it off.

    The cartridge or seat in the valve is worn. The valve sits inside the wall, so we open the trim and replace the insert.

  • Temperature swings.

    A pressure-balancing or thermostatic cartridge is failing. This is also a scald risk and worth fixing promptly.

  • Water drips from the tub spout during a shower.

    The diverter, the part that sends water up to the showerhead, is worn or the spout is loose.

  • Handle spins without effect.

    The stem or handle adapter is stripped.

  • Stain on the ceiling below a bathroom.

    Check the valve, the spout seal and the drain before assuming a pipe has burst.

Supply valves: the small part that decides how bad a leak gets

Every sink and toilet should have a shutoff valve, often called a stop or angle stop, so you can isolate one fixture. Old multi-turn valves can seize after years of sitting open, then leak or snap when someone finally turns them.

We often replace the stop at the same time as the faucet, since a seized valve defeats the point of having one. Quarter-turn ball valves are easier to use and less prone to sticking. If a valve cannot be saved, the fix may mean shutting the main for a short time, which is another reason to find your main valve now.

Hose bibs and cold weather

An outdoor hose bib that drips, or leaks inside the wall when the hose is on, is common after winter. Disconnect hoses before freezing weather, because a hose left attached can trap water in the pipe behind the spigot. A frost-free bib has a long stem that puts the shutoff inside the heated wall.

St. Louis Public Radio reported a wind chill near minus 33 degrees Fahrenheit in the region on the night of December 22 to 23, 2022. In January 2025, the City of Wildwood posted that frozen pipes become more common as temperatures drop. So check outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze, and look at them again in spring.

What affects the price of faucet repair

A cartridge swap and a shower valve replacement are very different jobs. These factors move the cost.

Type and brand of faucet.

Common cartridges are cheap and widely stocked, while specialty or imported fixtures cost more.

Access.

A valve behind tile needs an opening, which adds time and finish repair.

Condition of the shutoff valves.

A seized stop must be replaced before the faucet work can begin.

Fixture you supply.

Customer-provided faucets save on parts but we cannot vouch for a part we did not choose.

Time of the call.

Night and weekend visits cost more than scheduled ones.

A sudden pipe or valve failure that damages floors may be covered, while a slow drip over months often is not. Your policy decides.

To get a clear price before work starts, call us and tell us the faucet brand if you can read it.
