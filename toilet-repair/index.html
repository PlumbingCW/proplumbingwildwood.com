Signs your toilet needs a plumber

Most toilet complaints start with one of these symptoms.

  • It runs after the flush.

    Water keeps trickling into the bowl or the tank refills every few minutes. A worn flapper or a stuck fill valve is the usual cause.

  • Water pools around the base.

    A failed wax ring, a loose closet bolt or a cracked flange lets water seep out at the floor.

  • It wobbles or rocks.

    A loose toilet has already stressed its seal, even if the floor is still dry.

  • The handle feels loose or sticks.

    A broken chain or lift arm inside the tank stops the flush.

  • It gurgles or backs up.

    If other drains in the house slow down too, the blockage may be past the toilet.

  • It flushes weakly.

    Mineral buildup under the rim or low tank water can cut the flush.

Running toilet: finding the leak in the tank

A running toilet is the most common call, and it wastes water without any visible mess. Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

If the water rises to the top of the overflow tube and keeps running, the fill valve is not shutting off. Both parts are inexpensive and many homeowners replace them, but older tanks sometimes have corroded bolts or hard-to-find parts that make a full toilet replacement the better choice.

Missouri American Water's 2023 report shows total hardness of 66 to 150 ppm at its Meramec River plants and 82 to 226 ppm at its Missouri River plants. We cannot tell which plant feeds your address. Mineral scale on a fill valve is more common in hard water, so if parts keep failing, test your tap.

The age of the toilet and the cause of the problem decide this. Here is how we usually weigh it.

Cracked porcelain cannot be repaired. Water between the tank and bowl or from the bowl itself means replacement.

Urgent situation

When a toilet leak or overflow is an emergency

Treat it as urgent if the toilet is overflowing and the water will not stop, if water is leaking through a ceiling below, or if sewage is rising from the bowl. Shut the supply valve behind the toilet and, for an overflow, lift the tank lid and press the flapper closed.

Keep children and pets away from the floor, since sewage carries bacteria. Do not run the washing machine or other fixtures if the home drains to the same line. Take photos of the damage for your insurer or landlord, because your policy decides what is covered.

Night and weekend calls are routine for us, and same-day appointments are offered when available. The sooner we shut off the source, the less water reaches the subfloor.

Wax ring and flange: what holds a toilet to the floor

A toilet sits on a flange, a ring fixed to the floor over the drain pipe. A wax ring, or a modern wax-free equivalent, forms the seal between the toilet and the flange. If the flange is cracked, rusted or sitting below the finished floor, a new ring will not hold.

That is why we lift the toilet before promising a fix. If the flange is damaged, we repair or replace it. If the subfloor has softened from a slow leak, it may need repair first, and a moisture meter helps us see how far the water went.

Planning a new toilet installation

Replacing a toilet is a good time to choose a better one. Look for the EPA WaterSense label, which means 1.28 gallons per flush or less. Measure the rough-in, the distance from the wall to the center of the flange, because it limits which models fit.

A few other choices matter: round or elongated bowl, height, and whether the tank bolts to the bowl. Tell us what you picked, or ask us to bring options.
