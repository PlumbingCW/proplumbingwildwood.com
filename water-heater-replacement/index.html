Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Wildwood, MO

Water heater replacement in Wildwood, MO is the removal of a failed or worn-out tank or tankless unit and the installation of a new one that fits your fuel, space, venting and household demand. When a tank has failed with water on the floor, shut the cold supply valve, cut power or gas to the unit and call. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so a Saturday night leak gets answered.

Why many Wildwood heaters are due now

Census Bureau estimates for 2020 to 2024 give 1992 as the median year Wildwood homes were built. About 42.8 percent of the city's 13,391 housing units predate 1990. A heater original to a 1992 house would be over 30 years old, which is far past typical tank life, so the unit in your house is almost certainly a replacement.

What that means for you: the label date is a better predictor than how the water feels. A tank installed by a builder or a prior owner in the 2010s may be right at the point where failure risk climbs. Replacing on your schedule costs less stress than replacing at 2 a.m. with water on the floor.

When a failed water heater is an emergency

A tank that has started pouring water, a unit that smells of gas or a heater that is hot to the touch with no hot water at the taps all call for action. Close the cold water valve at the top of the tank. Switch off the breaker or turn the gas control to off, and keep people and pets away from a wet floor near an electric unit.

Choosing the type of replacement

Like-for-like gas or electric tank

The simplest swap keeps the same fuel, size and location. Existing gas lines, venting, power circuit and water connections can often be reused, which keeps the job short. Many people choose this when the old tank just wore out and nothing about the household has changed.

Heat pump (hybrid) electric

The Department of Energy describes heat pump water heaters as markedly more efficient than standard electric resistance tanks, because they move heat from the surrounding air instead of generating it directly. They need clear airflow and a place to drain condensate. Manufacturers specify minimum room volume and temperature range, so a small closet may not suit one.

Moving from gas to a heat pump

Some households ask about replacing a gas tank with an electric heat pump unit. That change usually needs a dedicated 240 volt circuit, a condensate path, and a decision about capping or reusing the gas line and flue. It is a planned job, not a same-hour swap, and we explain the full scope before you commit.

Tankless

Tankless units heat water on demand and need a gas line and venting sized to match, or a large electric circuit. They are a bigger upgrade than a tank swap, and our tankless water heater page covers that route in detail.

What gets checked before the new unit goes in

A good replacement is more than lifting out one tank and setting another. These items are checked each time.

  • Size and recovery.

    A household of five needs a different tank than one or two people. The rating label gives capacity and recovery rate, and we match both to your hot-water use.

  • Venting on gas units.

    The flue has to carry combustion gases outside. We check slope, connections and draft, and test for backdrafting after the swap.

  • Relief valve and discharge line.

    The temperature and pressure relief valve needs a proper discharge pipe. This is a safety device, not an extra.

  • Expansion control.

    Where the house has a check valve or pressure reducing valve, heated water has nowhere to expand, so an expansion tank may be needed.

  • Shutoffs and flex lines.

    Old gate valves that will not close and brittle connectors are replaced rather than reused.

  • Drain pan and floor drain.

    A pan under a unit in a finished space limits the damage the next time something leaks.

Permit questions and water quality

Permit rules vary, so ask the Wildwood building department before replacing a water heater. The city's permit page lists new or replaced plumbing among work needing a building permit, and the city has adopted the St. Louis County Plumbing Code (Chapter 1103, adopted 2019). We cannot say whether a specific swap needs one.

Missouri American Water supplies the city with treated surface water. Its 2023 report lists hardness from 66 to 150 ppm at Meramec plants and 82 to 226 ppm at Missouri River plants. Homes on private wells need their own test. Scale shortens heater life, so a water softener is worth considering with a new tank.

What affects the price of a water heater replacement

We do not publish prices on this page, because the unit and the site conditions drive the bill. You get a clear price before work starts.

Type and size.

A 40 gallon tank, an 80 gallon tank, a heat pump unit and a tankless model sit in very different price classes.

Fuel change.

Switching between gas and electric adds circuit, gas line, flue or condensate work.

Venting and code updates.

A flue that no longer meets current St. Louis County Plumbing Code requirements must be corrected as part of the job.

Access.

Tight basements, attics and stairs add labor, as does hauling the old tank out.

Timing.

Night and weekend emergency work can cost more than a scheduled daytime install, and we state that first.

A tank that fails from age or corrosion is generally a maintenance cost, while water damage from a sudden burst may be covered. Your policy decides, so ask the insurer before discarding the old tank.
