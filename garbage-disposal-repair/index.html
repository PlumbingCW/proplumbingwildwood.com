Jam, leak or dead unit: sorting out what you have

Three different failures look alike from the sink. The sound and the location of water tell most of the story.

Urgent situation

When a disposal leak or kitchen backup is an emergency

Treat it as urgent if water is flowing from under the sink, sewage-smelling water is coming up through the sink or dishwasher, or the unit is sparking or smoking. Turn off the disposal switch and the breaker, and close the supply valves under the sink if water is running.

Keep the cabinet empty and mop up water quickly, since particleboard cabinets swell and warp when wet. Photograph the leak and the damage before cleanup, because your policy decides what is covered and your insurer may ask.

We answer on the 24/7 line at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are offered when available. If the problem is only a hum, it can usually wait for a scheduled visit.

Safe steps for a jammed disposal

Many jams clear in a few minutes. Follow these steps in order, with the power off.

  • Turn off the switch and unplug the unit.

    If it is wired in, switch off the breaker. A disposal that starts by surprise can injure you.

  • Look with a flashlight.

    Use tongs or pliers to lift out visible objects. Never use your fingers.

  • Turn the blades by hand.

    Many units have a hex socket at the bottom. A hex wrench turned back and forth frees a stuck impeller.

  • Press the red reset button.

    It sits on the bottom of the unit and trips when the motor overheats.

  • Test with cold water running.

    If it still hums or leaks, stop and call.

Where disposals leak

A disposal has three common leak points. The top flange where the sink meets the unit can weep when the putty or gasket dries out. The dishwasher inlet and the drain outlet connect to hoses and pipe that can loosen. A leak from the body or the seam near the bottom means the shell has failed, and the unit is finished.

A flashlight and a dry paper towel help pinpoint the source. Run water for a minute and watch each joint. If the water appears at the body or from the housing seam, repair is not worth the money, and replacement is the honest recommendation.

Electrical faults: what a plumber checks

A silent disposal is not always dead. We check the reset button, the switch and the outlet first, then whether the unit has power. If power arrives and the motor does not respond, the motor or its start capacitor has failed.

Wiring behind a disposal switch is electrical work, and a licensed electrician should handle any fault in the circuit itself. If you are unsure whether the problem is the unit or the circuit, we can tell you which trade to call. If your unit runs from a countertop air switch, that button can fail too.

Adding or replacing a disposal in a Wildwood kitchen

Installing a new disposal means mounting the flange, connecting the drain and, if you have a dishwasher, opening its knockout plug so the dishwasher hose can drain through the unit. Forgetting the plug is a common reason a dishwasher fills with water after a new install.

St. Louis County guidance has said a plumbing permit is required to replace or reset sinks and to install dishwashers when countertops or cabinets are replaced. That flyer is dated 2013, so confirm current rules with the building department. A like-for-like disposal swap is a different job, and we can explain how it fits.

Septic and package-plant homes need extra thought. The city's Master Plan says much of Wildwood's soil is unsuitable for individual septic systems and that some subdivisions use package treatment plants. Food waste adds solids to those systems, so ask your septic or treatment provider whether a disposal is a good idea.
