When you need an emergency plumber in Wildwood right now

Some plumbing problems cannot sit until morning. Water spreading across a finished basement, sewage coming up through a floor drain, a pipe that let go inside a wall and a water heater tank pouring onto the floor all belong here. If you are searching for an emergency plumber because nobody else can come tonight, start with a phone call instead of more searching.

The dispatch line is open nights, weekends and holidays. The dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, whether you can reach the shutoff and which part of the house is affected, then routes the job to a licensed plumber. That short conversation is how a 24/7 emergency plumber service decides who goes to your address first.

We do not promise an arrival time on the phone, because traffic on Highway 109 and Manchester Road, the plumber's current job and the weather all change it. If your problem can safely wait for a daytime visit, we will tell you that instead of pushing a middle-of-the-night call.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

These steps limit damage and keep everyone safe until help arrives.

  • Shut off the main water valve.

    It is usually where the line enters the house, often in a basement or utility room near the water heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If your home runs on a private well, the shutoff may sit near the pressure tank.

  • Stop using drains and toilets.

    When sewage or gray water is rising, every flush, shower or washer cycle sends more into the house. Keep children and pets out of the affected area.

  • Stay clear of wet electrical areas.

    Do not stand in water to reach a panel, and do not touch outlets or cords that are wet. If you see sparks or smell burning, leave and call 911.

  • Close the cold water valve on a leaking water heater.

    It sits on the pipe above the tank. If the heater is electric, switch off its breaker only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor.

  • Photograph the damage and move belongings.

    Pictures of the leak, the wet areas and any damaged items help with an insurance claim or a landlord report. Lift valuables off wet floors.

  • Skip the chemical drain cleaner.

    It rarely clears a deep blockage, and splashing caustic liquid at a plumber working on the line is a hazard. Wait for the plumber instead.

The plumbing services we dispatch in Wildwood

Most calls fall into a few groups. Clogs and slow lines go to drain cleaning, and root-filled or greasy lines may need hydro jetting. Sewage in the lowest drain of the house points to sewer line cleaning or, if the pipe itself has failed, sewer line repair.

Water that should not be moving calls for burst pipe repair when a pipe has failed, leak detection when the meter keeps spinning and you cannot see why, and water line repair when the trouble sits between the meter and the house. Tanks that leak or quit are handled through water heater repair and water heater replacement.

Fixture jobs such as toilet repair, faucet repair and garbage disposal repair are usually daytime work. Wet basements bring in sump pump repair, and shops and offices can use commercial plumbing. The full list is on our services page.

Wells, hills and 1990s houses: what Wildwood plumbing looks like

Missouri American Water supplies much of Wildwood, and the city's FAQ points sewer questions to MSD Project Clear. The city's master plan also says many property owners west of State Route 109, and some to the east, are on well water, and that development outside MSD boundaries lacks public sewer. So a pressure tank or well pump problem is a different job from a water main break, and a septic or subdivision treatment plant is not an MSD line.

The master plan describes the city as rolling, hilly woodland on the Ozark foothills, with soils that are sensitive to erosion. Hillside lots often mean basements below grade, and a sump pump that quits during a hard rain can flood a lower level quickly. Tell the dispatcher if your house sits on a slope.

Census data puts the median Wildwood home at 1992, and about 42.8 percent of the city's housing units were built before 1990. In houses that age, the first water heater has often come and gone, braided supply lines under sinks and toilets are decades old and drain lines carry years of grease or roots. Those three are the first places to look when an older home develops a leak or a slow drain.

Cold snaps and frozen pipes

In January 2025 the City of Wildwood warned residents that as temperatures drop, the likelihood of water line issues increases, with frozen pipes becoming more common. If a pipe on an exterior wall stops delivering water during a hard freeze, shut the main valve before it thaws, then call. A frozen pipe that cracks releases its water only when the ice melts.

What we do, and what we do not do

We dispatch licensed plumbers for drains, sewer lines, water heaters, leaks, burst pipes, fixtures, sump pumps and commercial plumbing. We are licensed and insured, we give a clear price before work starts and we do not publish prices on this site because a price depends on what the plumber finds.

We do not handle gas leaks, structural foundation repair, water damage drying or repairs to drywall and flooring, and we do not pump septic tanks or run subdivision treatment plants. If you smell gas, leave the building and call Spire or 911 before you call a plumber. Whether your insurance covers a loss is up to your policy, not to us.

Plumbing in Wildwood falls under the St. Louis County Plumbing Code as adopted by the city. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Wildwood building department before a big project. To check any plumber's licence, see how to do it on our about page.
